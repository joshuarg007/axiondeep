TTS Script

Most chatbots ask one question. If the visitor says no, the bot gives up. That is a waste.

Site2CRM AI is goal-driven. When a visitor pushes back, the AI does not quit. It switches angles.

On soft persistence, the bot makes up to three gentle attempts. It respects rejection quickly and keeps the tone light.

On medium, it uses the full rebuttal count you configure — up to ten. Each attempt brings a new value proposition. A different reason to engage.

On aggressive, the AI becomes a closer. It creates urgency. Digs into objections. Offers alternatives. If the visitor will not share an email, it asks for a phone number. If they will not book a demo, it offers a free resource. It cycles through tactics until the configured limit.

And the capture happens automatically. The AI watches every message for patterns. The moment someone types an email address, a phone number, or says their name, it is extracted and stored. No form submission needed. No button click.

The email regex catches standard formats. The phone parser handles dashes, parentheses, spaces, and raw ten-digit numbers. The name extractor reads patterns like "I am Sarah" or "My name is John" or "John here."

When enough contact info is captured — email plus name, or phone plus name — the lead is created in your CRM. A webhook fires. Your team gets notified.

The visitor never filled out a form. They just had a conversation.

TTS Script

Every startup has dozens of logins. Your bank. Your state tax portal. Stripe. AWS. Your registered agent. Your insurance provider. Your domain registrar. QuickBooks. Gusto. The list keeps growing.

Most founders store these in a spreadsheet. Or a sticky note. Or their browser's saved passwords with no master key.

Made4Founders has a built-in credential vault. And it is not an afterthought. It uses AES-256-GCM encryption — the same standard used by banks and governments. The key derivation uses Argon2id with three iterations, sixty-four megabytes of memory, and four parallel threads. This is the gold standard for password hashing. Your master password never leaves your device. We physically cannot see your credentials.

Here is the setup. First time you open the vault, you create a master password. Minimum eight characters. You see a clear warning — if you forget this password, your credentials cannot be recovered. This is real encryption, not a reversible encoding.

Once set, the vault locks automatically after one hour of inactivity. Come back and you see the lock screen. Enter your master password. If you have TOTP enabled, you enter your six-digit authenticator code too. That is three-factor authentication. Your session, your master password, and your authenticator.

Inside the vault, you see your credentials organized by category. Nine categories. Banking. Tax. Legal. Government. Accounting. Insurance. Vendors. Tools. And Other. Each one has its own color and icon.

The vault recognizes over one hundred and fifty services. Type in AWS and it matches the logo automatically. Same for Stripe, HubSpot, Chase, GitHub, Salesforce, Gusto — all the services a startup actually uses. AI tools, cloud providers, CRMs, payment processors, social platforms, analytics, databases, domain registrars. All matched.

Adding a credential takes seconds. Enter the name. The URL. Pick a category. Add your username and password. Add notes if you want. Set a purpose field to remind yourself what this login is for.

And then there are the additional fields. This is where it gets serious. You can store API keys, client secrets, bearer tokens, OAuth tokens, SSH private keys, encryption keys, Stripe publishable and secret keys, webhook secrets, PINs, security questions and answers, recovery codes, and backup codes. Each field is typed — text, secret, URL, date, or dropdown. Every single one is encrypted individually with its own random nonce.

View your credentials in list mode or grid mode. Search by name or URL. Filter by category. Filter by business if you manage multiple companies. Sort by name, category, or date. Mark favorites for quick access — they show in a pinned section at the top.

Click any credential to open the detail panel. Everything is masked by default. Dots across the screen. Click the eye icon to reveal. Click copy to put the password on your clipboard — it calls the server, decrypts on the fly, and writes to your clipboard. The button turns green for two seconds to confirm.

The vault is not a feature. It is a statement. Your credentials belong to you. Not to a browser. Not to a spreadsheet. And not to us.

The data room organizes documents into folders for due diligence. Create shareable links with passwords, expiration dates, and access limits. See who viewed what and when.

Investor updates pull your latest MRR, ARR, runway, burn rate, and customer count into an email template. Send it to your cap table shareholders. Track opens and clicks. What used to take two hours takes five minutes.
